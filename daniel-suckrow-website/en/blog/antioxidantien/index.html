Antioxidants: What they really do and why variety matters more than capsules

By Daniel Suckrow · Holistic nutritional advice

Antioxidants are often advertised as a shield against free radicals and rapid ageing. The truth is more nuanced: your body has its own, well-functioning protective system, and free radicals are not only harmful. This article shows what lies behind the term, why capsules usually do nothing and which foods you should really favour.

Free radicals: not just enemies

Reactive oxygen compounds, often called free radicals, are formed during completely normal metabolic processes. They react very easily with other molecules and can alter proteins, fats and even genetic material in the process.

At the same time, they fulfil important tasks. Your immune system uses them, for example, to kill pathogens. They also serve as signals that help cells adapt to stress. That is one reason why exercise is so healthy in the long run: it briefly produces more free radicals, and the body responds with stronger protective systems.

Oxidative stress only occurs when the formation of reactive molecules permanently exceeds the body’s ability to neutralise them and repair damage.

How your body protects itself

Your body does not rely only on antioxidants from food. It has a whole network of its own protective substances and enzymes, for example glutathione, superoxide dismutase and catalase. These systems can be finely regulated and adapt to the current load.

Further helpers come from food:

  • Vitamin C and vitamin E neutralise reactive molecules directly.
  • Selenium, zinc and manganese are building blocks of the body’s own protective enzymes.
  • Phytochemicals such as polyphenols, carotenoids and anthocyanins act in many ways, often rather as signals for the body’s own protective systems.

Smoking, a lot of alcohol, air pollution, constant stress and lack of sleep can disturb the balance. Tackling these usually achieves more than any capsule.

Do antioxidant supplements help?

The evidence here is sobering. Large studies with high-dose supplements of vitamin C, vitamin E or beta-carotene were unable to prevent cardiovascular disease, cancer or early death. In some cases they even showed harm.

Important: In studies, beta-carotene supplements increased the risk of lung cancer in smokers. During cancer treatment, too, you should only take high-dose antioxidants after consulting your treatment team, as they can weaken the effect of some therapies.

Why do foods work but capsules do not? One explanation is that in fruit and vegetables hundreds of different substances work together, in natural amounts and together with fibre. An isolated substance in a high dose, on the other hand, can disturb the finely tuned balance. Supplements therefore mainly make sense when a deficiency has been proven.

What do ORAC values tell you?

You may already have come across lists of ORAC values with aronia berries, spices or cocoa at the top. ORAC is a laboratory test. It measures how well a sample slows down a certain oxidation reaction in a test tube.

These values cannot be transferred to your body, however. Digestion, absorption and metabolism change the substances considerably. The US Department of Agriculture therefore withdrew its ORAC database as early as 2012. There is no recognised daily requirement of 5,000 or 7,000 ORAC units.

The best sources: colourful and varied

Instead of chasing individual “top performers”, variety is what pays off most. Each colour stands for different phytochemicals. Good sources are:

  • Berries and dark fruits such as blueberries, blackberries, aronia and cherries. They provide plenty of anthocyanins.
  • Green vegetables such as kale, broccoli and spinach. They contain vitamin C, carotenoids and folate.
  • Orange and red vegetables such as carrots, pumpkin, peppers and tomatoes. They are full of carotenoids.
  • Pulses, wholegrains, nuts and seeds. They provide vitamin E, selenium, zinc and polyphenols.
  • Herbs and spices such as turmeric, ginger, oregano and cinnamon, as well as green tea and cocoa.

A practical example

Aim to eat at least five different colours every day. Oats with blueberries and walnuts in the morning, a bowl with lentils, peppers, carrots and rocket at lunch, roasted vegetables with pumpkin, broccoli and red onions in the evening. That way you get a huge range of protective substances along the way, without needing a single capsule.

Tip: Whole fruits are better than juice. They contain more fibre, are more filling and make blood sugar rise more slowly.

More than food: What else strengthens your protection

Your body’s own protective system can be trained. These habits often have a stronger effect than any single food:

  • Regular exercise, ideally a mix of endurance and strength. It stimulates the formation of the body’s own protective enzymes.
  • Seven to nine hours of sleep. Many repair processes take place at night.
  • Not smoking. Tobacco smoke is one of the strongest sources of oxidative stress there is.
  • Little or no alcohol. Breaking it down produces reactive compounds that put a strain on cells.
  • Breaks and relaxation. Constant stress keeps the body on alert and promotes inflammation.

A capsule cannot make up for these basics. By starting here, you support your body at the root.

And what about milk thistle and the like?

Plant extracts such as milk thistle, green tea extract or high-dose curcumin often show impressive effects in the lab. In humans the results are mostly mixed. A reliable protective effect on the liver has not been proven for milk thistle, for example.

Extracts are also not automatically harmless. High-dose green tea extracts can damage the liver in rare cases, and some extracts influence the effect of medicines. As foods in normal amounts, green tea, turmeric and the like are a good addition, on the other hand.

Conclusion

Free radicals are part of life, and your body has a strong protective system of its own. In studies, high-dose antioxidant supplements usually bring no benefit and can sometimes even do harm. Laboratory ORAC values say little about the effect in your body. You protect yourself best with a colourful, plant-focused diet, exercise, good sleep and not smoking. Variety on your plate beats any capsule.
