Colon cleansing and gut restoration: What really helps your gut

By Daniel Suckrow · Holistic nutritional advice

Gut cures with healing clay, zeolite and psyllium husks promise to remove old deposits and toxins from the gut. Afterwards a probiotic is supposed to rebuild the gut flora. That sounds logical, but has little to do with reality. This article shows what is behind the promises and what is actually good for your gut.

What your gut does every day

Your gut breaks down food, absorbs nutrients and water and excretes what cannot be digested. To do this, the lining of the small intestine has folds, villi and tiny protrusions. Spread out, it would cover the area of a small flat.

Trillions of bacteria live in the large intestine, the so-called microbiome. They break down fibre, make important substances such as short-chain fatty acids and train your immune system. A large part of your immune cells sits in the gut wall.

And the best part: your gut cleans itself. The cells of the gut lining renew themselves about every three to five days. Wave-like movements continuously move the contents along. The liver and kidneys take care of breakdown products and harmful substances.

Is there such a thing as a “dirty” gut?

The idea that sugar, stress or animal foods cause waste or deposits to build up in the villi is widespread. It has not been scientifically proven. In colonoscopies, where doctors look into thousands of guts every day, no such deposits are found.

Bloating is not a sign of toxins passing from the gut into the blood either. It usually arises quite normally when gut bacteria ferment fibre, from swallowed air or from changes in gut movement. If complaints last longer, however, you should have them checked by a doctor.

Healing clay, zeolite and bentonite

Healing clay, zeolite and bentonite are clay minerals. In the lab they can bind certain substances to their surface. But that does not mean they specifically remove toxins, fungi or deposits in the gut. There are no robust studies showing a benefit for healthy people.

Important: Clay minerals can also bind medicines and nutrients and reduce their absorption. Some products may also be contaminated with heavy metals. I therefore advise against self-administered cures lasting several weeks. If you take medicines regularly, be sure to consult a doctor beforehand.

Healing clay can help with occasional heartburn in the short term. But that is something different from a “cleanse”.

Psyllium husks: useful, but not a brush

Psyllium husks contain soluble fibre that swells strongly with water. They make stools softer and bulkier and can help well with constipation. With diarrhoea, too, they can regulate stools and lower cholesterol slightly.

But they do not “scrub” the gut clean. They act like a natural fibre and are partly fermented by gut bacteria.

  • Start with one teaspoon a day and increase slowly.
  • Drink at least one large glass of water per teaspoon, never swallow them dry.
  • Keep a gap of at least one to two hours from medicines.
  • With swallowing difficulties, suspected bowel obstruction or severe abdominal pain, please consult a doctor first.

Do you need a probiotic to restore your gut?

A fixed sequence of several weeks of cleansing followed by recolonisation with a probiotic is not scientifically established. Your microbiome is not an empty garden that needs replanting. It is a living ecosystem that changes mainly through your diet.

Probiotics can help in certain situations, for example with diarrhoea during antibiotic treatment or with some forms of irritable bowel syndrome. But the effect depends heavily on the particular bacterial strain. A product with as many strains as possible is not automatically better. There is no general recommendation for healthy people.

What really helps your gut

  • Fibre: aim for at least 30 grams a day from pulses, wholegrains, vegetables, fruit, nuts and seeds. Increase slowly.
  • Variety: studies show that people who eat 30 different plant foods per week have a more diverse microbiome.
  • Fermented foods such as sauerkraut, kimchi, kombucha or soya yoghurt with live cultures can further promote diversity.
  • Drink enough, exercise regularly and sleep well.
  • Reduce stress. The gut and the brain are closely connected.

A practical example

Instead of a six-week gut cure, you try the following for four weeks: a portion of pulses every day, oats with flaxseed in the morning, some sauerkraut or kimchi twice a week and a short walk at lunchtime. You count how many different plant foods you eat per week and try to reach 30. That costs less than any cure and has been shown to support your microbiome.

Tip: Always have warning signs such as blood in the stool, unintended weight loss, persistent pain or prolonged changes in bowel habits checked by a doctor. A gut cure does not replace a diagnosis.

Conclusion

A healthy gut does not need to be cleansed. The idea of waste and deposits has not been scientifically proven. Healing clay, zeolite and bentonite bring healthy people no proven benefit and can even bind medicines. Psyllium husks help with constipation, probiotics only in certain situations. What really strengthens your gut is a high-fibre, varied plant-based diet, complemented by exercise, sleep and less stress.
