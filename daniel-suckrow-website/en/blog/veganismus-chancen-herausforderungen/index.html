Living fully plant-based: An honest look at the opportunities and challenges

By Daniel Suckrow · Holistic nutritional advice

Hardly any nutrition topic is discussed as emotionally as giving up animal products completely. For some it is the solution for health, the climate and animal welfare, for others a risky form of malnutrition. As so often, the truth lies somewhere in between. This article takes an honest look at both sides and shows what matters if you want to live fully plant-based.

More than a way of eating

For many people, a fully plant-based lifestyle is more than a meal plan. As far as is practically possible, they avoid all products for which animals are used, including leather, wool or cosmetics with animal ingredients. The motives vary: animal welfare and ethics, climate and environment, health or a mix of everything.

The distinction is important: a fully plant-based diet is not automatically healthy. Chips, crisps and cola are plant-based too. And a plant-focused diet with occasional animal products can also be very healthy. What matters is what ends up on your plate.

The opportunities

Health: a well-planned plant-based diet with plenty of vegetables, fruit, pulses, wholegrains, nuts and seeds provides lots of fibre and little saturated fat. In studies, people who eat this way have, on average, lower cholesterol levels, lower blood pressure, a lower body weight and a lower risk of type 2 diabetes and coronary heart disease. In 2025, the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, the world’s largest association of nutrition professionals, confirmed that well-planned fully plant-based diets can meet adults’ needs and offer health benefits.

Environment: on average, plant foods cause considerably fewer greenhouse gases and need much less land and water than animal foods. Switching is among the most effective things you can do for the climate as an individual.

Animals: every year, more than 80 billion land animals are slaughtered for food worldwide, most of them from intensive farming. Anyone who goes without animal products directly reduces demand.

The challenges

Nutrients: you always have to supplement vitamin B12. Iodine, omega-3, vitamin D, calcium, iron, zinc and selenium need attention. With some knowledge this is very doable, but without planning gaps can arise.

Bones: a large British study found a somewhat higher risk of fractures in people who ate fully plant-based. A lower intake of calcium and protein and a lower body weight probably play a role. Calcium-rich mineral water, fortified plant drinks, tofu, enough pulses, vitamin D and strength training counteract this.

Everyday life: eating out, family celebrations and travelling can be challenging at first. The time to cook and the necessary knowledge cannot be taken for granted by everyone either.

Processing and sustainability: many substitute products are highly processed and contain a lot of salt. And plant products can be problematic too, for example palm oil from cleared rainforests or high water use in dry regions.

Important: During pregnancy, breastfeeding and for children and teenagers, a fully plant-based diet is possible, but it needs particularly careful planning and ideally professional support. Here mistakes have faster and stronger consequences.

Step by step instead of all at once

Many people do not fail for lack of knowledge, but because the switch is too radical. I recommend a gradual approach. That way you get to know new recipes, and your gut can get used to all the fibre.

A practical example

In the first two weeks you change your breakfast, for example to oats with plant drink, berries and nuts. Then come two to three plant-based main meals a week, such as a lentil curry, a bean stir-fry and a tofu bowl. At the same time you start taking vitamin B12 and cook with iodised salt. After two to three months you have enough favourite recipes to switch completely, if you want to. A blood check after about a year shows you whether everything is on track.

Conclusion

A fully plant-based lifestyle offers real opportunities for health, the environment and animals. But it does not run itself. You always have to supplement vitamin B12, and iodine, omega-3, vitamin D, calcium and protein need attention. Anyone who mainly eats unprocessed foods, keeps an eye on the critical nutrients and proceeds step by step can live very well and healthily on plants. And even if you do not switch completely, you benefit from every extra plant-based dish on your plate.
