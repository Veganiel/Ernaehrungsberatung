Challenge 06 · Twelve weeks

Half marathon training plan for beginners and advanced runners

Twelve weeks to 21.1 km: one plan for your first half marathon and one for a new personal best – with long runs, clear pace guidance, recovery weeks and tips on fuelling and recovery.

06

21.1 km with a plan

Twelve weeks with recovery and taper – for your first finish or your next personal best.

12 weeks · 2 levels

How a training week is structured

3–5 runs a weekLong run up to 120 min80% easy
Easy runs · the foundationMost of your kilometres are run at a conversational pace. They build endurance and spare your tendons and joints.
One quality session per weekFaster sections, intervals or race-pace running make you quicker. Always with a 10–15 min warm-up and a 10 min cool-down.
The long run · once a weekIt grows week by week and makes the distance familiar. Pace: easy, and slower than you think is fine.
Rest days and strengthAt least one rest day after hard sessions. Two short strength sessions a week help prevent overload.
80 / 20 as a rule of thumb: About 80% of your running time is easy and at most 20% fast or hard. Running every session fast often makes you tired rather than faster.Find your easy heart-rate zone with the calculator →
YOUR TRAINING PLAN

Choose your level

Both plans last twelve weeks, with recovery in weeks 4 and 8 and a lighter final phase before the race. Repeat a week if it feels too hard.

UNDERSTANDING PACE

What the terms in the plan mean

You do not need a heart-rate watch – your own sense of effort is perfectly enough to start with.

01

Easy

You can talk in full sentences. This is the pace for most runs and for the long run.

02

Brisk

Noticeably faster; you can only speak in short sentences. For short sections of one to five minutes.

03

Threshold

Comfortably hard: you could hold this pace for about an hour but can only say a few words.

04

Race pace

The pace you are aiming for in your 10 km or half marathon. In training you only run it in sections.

Training with heart rate?If you use a heart-rate watch: easy runs are usually in zone 2, threshold sections in zone 4. You can estimate your zones with the heart-rate zone calculator.

What matters most

01

Practise fuelling

On runs longer than 75 to 90 minutes, water and easily digestible carbohydrates can help. Test in training what your stomach tolerates.

02

Test shoes and clothing

Only race in shoes and clothing you have already worn on long training runs.

03

Keep the first half calm

The most common half marathon mistake is starting too fast. Run evenly and pick up the pace at the end.

Safety firstIf you have been inactive for a long time, have a medical condition (for example of the heart or circulation) or take regular medication, get medical advice before you start. Stop immediately if you experience chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, dizziness or palpitations. Pain that gets worse while running or is still there the next morning is a reason to take a break – if it persists, have it checked. This challenge is not medical advice.

Questions and answers

How long should you train for a half marathon?

If you can run 10 km comfortably, plan about twelve weeks of focused training. The long run gradually builds up to around 100 to 120 minutes.

Do I need to run 21 km in training?

No. A long run of 16 to 18 kilometres is enough preparation. You cover the rest on race day with the freshness from your taper.
