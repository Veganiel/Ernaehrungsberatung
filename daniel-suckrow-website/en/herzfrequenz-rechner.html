Calculate heart rate zones with optional resting HR

Your heart rate zone calculator: enter your age and optionally your resting heart rate and a reliably measured maximum heart rate to explore four estimated training ranges.

✳

Your details

The Tanaka formula used here applies to healthy adults and does not differentiate by sex. This selection therefore does not change the result.

Without resting HR: percentage of maximum HR. With resting HR: Karvonen heart rate reserve. Measure resting HR while relaxed, preferably on several mornings. Only enter a reliably measured maximum HR, not an isolated watch peak. A measured maximum replaces the age estimate. Input limits are technical plausibility checks.

Please check your inputs: age 18–100, optional resting HR 30–120 and measured maximum HR 100–250, all whole numbers. Resting HR must be below the maximum HR used.

Your guide

Estimated maximum heart rate

Tanaka formula: 208 − 0.7 × age
187 beats/min

This calculator uses four bands of 10 percentage points of maximum HR or heart rate reserve, depending on the method. Other training models may use different boundaries.

60–70% of HRmax

131–150 beats/min

Train at high intensity

80–90% of HRmax
150–168 beats/min

High effort; only a few words at a time are possible. Best suited to short intense efforts with appropriate training experience. Allow time for recovery.

Combine heart rate with how you feel

“Zone 2” is not defined identically in every model. The calculated 60–70% is a rough guide and does not identify your individual metabolic or lactate threshold. During easy training, you should still be able to speak in full sentences. Heat, stress, medication and training status can affect heart rate.

Below 50%, effort falls below the ranges shown here. Above 90%, effort is very high and is not a general training target of this calculator.

How accurate are the results?

All zones remain estimates and guidance. Karvonen accounts for resting heart rate but is not automatically more accurate. Equal percentages of maximum heart rate and heart rate reserve are not equivalent.

Smartwatches may offer more personalized estimates using individual data, but are not inherently more accurate. Sensor errors, different formulas and zone models can explain differences. Measuring your pulse alone does not measure your aerobic or anaerobic threshold.

Even a reliably measured maximum heart rate does not determine individual thresholds. Professionally interpreted lactate testing or cardiopulmonary exercise testing is better suited to this. Aerobic and anaerobic energy production overlap; zone boundaries are not switches.

How to interpret these numbers

Your actual maximum heart rate may differ considerably from this estimate. An age-based formula does not replace an individual exercise assessment. Rounded boundaries are approximations, not sharp transitions.

If you are new to exercise, start with light effort and progress gradually. If you have cardiovascular disease or take medication that affects heart rate, have a clinician determine your training ranges. Stop exercising and seek medical assessment if you experience chest pain, dizziness or unusual shortness of breath.

Questions and answers

When does this calculator use Karvonen?

With resting HR, the tool calculates ranges from heart rate reserve: resting HR plus intensity times the difference between maximum and resting HR. Without resting HR, it uses percentages of maximum HR.

Why does my smartwatch show different zones?

Watches may use different zone models, a different maximum HR or individual estimates. These calculator ranges are not measured aerobic or anaerobic thresholds.
